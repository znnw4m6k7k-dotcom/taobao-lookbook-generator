# Twelve-pose fashion lookbook library

Use this twelve-card library as a pose draw deck for the twelve person-centric shots in the fixed plan. Draw a coverage-compatible card for each person shot and avoid replacement/reuse where evidence allows. Do not apply these full-body poses to the four product-detail shots. Evidence compatibility takes priority over using every pose.

Record the selected `pose_id` in the shot prompt and manifest attempt. Do not invent a pocket, strap, accessory, or garment behavior merely to satisfy a pose. Skip or adapt a pose when it would hide a defining feature, distort the garment, or conflict with the assigned front/side/back view.

## 01 | Front full-body standing pose

Full-body front-facing standing pose. Both arms relaxed naturally at the sides, legs together or slightly staggered. The model stands upright with relaxed shoulders and looks directly at the camera. Keep the posture natural and effortless, clearly showing the front silhouette, fit, proportions, and details of the outfit.

## 02 | Side full-body standing pose

Full-body side standing pose. The body is turned approximately 45–90 degrees to the camera, while the head turns slightly toward the camera. Arms hang naturally at the sides or hold a supplied accessory in a relaxed position. Maintain a calm, effortless posture that clearly shows the side silhouette and proportions of the outfit.

## 03 | Back full-body standing pose

Full-body back-facing standing pose. The model stands with the back facing the camera, shoulders and neck relaxed, both arms naturally hanging at the sides, and legs straight in a comfortable position. Clearly display the back silhouette, garment construction, length, and overall proportions.

## 04 | Side pose with folded arms

Full-body side pose with the body turned sideways. Both arms are gently folded across the chest without creating tension or covering too much of the garment. The head turns slightly toward the camera. Keep the shoulders relaxed and the overall posture quiet, minimal, and natural. Do not use this pose when folded arms would hide a defining neckline, closure, print, or construction detail.

## 05 | Relaxed three-quarter turn

Full-body three-quarter standing pose. The body is positioned at approximately a three-quarter angle to the camera. One hand rests lightly near the hem or waist, while the other arm hangs naturally at the side. The body remains relaxed with a subtle shift in weight, creating an effortless fashion lookbook pose.

## 06 | Side pose with natural hand movement

Full-body side pose. The body is turned sideways while one hand is gently raised toward the chest, a supplied shoulder strap, neckline, or collar as if naturally adjusting the garment. The other arm remains relaxed. Keep the gesture subtle and quiet, avoiding exaggerated movement or excessive garment obstruction.

## 07 | Natural walking pose

The model walks naturally and slowly toward the camera, with one leg stepping forward and the other slightly behind to create subtle movement. Both arms swing naturally with the walking motion. Shoulders and neck remain relaxed. The model looks calmly toward the camera or slightly away from it. Avoid exaggerated strides and clearly display the front silhouette, hemline, and overall proportions of the outfit.

## 08 | Side walking pose

The body remains approximately 70–90 degrees sideways while the model walks naturally across the frame. One leg steps forward while the back leg extends naturally behind. Both arms create a subtle natural swing, and the head turns slightly toward the camera. Keep the movement controlled and effortless, emphasizing the side silhouette, waistline, and natural movement of the garment.

## 09 | Over-the-shoulder turning pose

The model stands with the body turned approximately 120–150 degrees away from the camera, creating a natural turning moment. One foot moves slightly backward or sideways while the upper body rotates subtly. The model turns the head naturally to look back toward the camera. Both arms remain relaxed, with one hand optionally resting near the thigh. Clearly show the back, side profile, and shoulder line of the outfit.

## 10 | One hand in pocket pose

Use only when an authoritative product or styling reference proves that the garment has a usable pocket. The model stands facing the camera or at a subtle three-quarter angle. Body weight rests naturally on one leg while the other leg extends slightly forward or sideways. One hand rests casually inside the verified pocket while the other arm hangs naturally at the side. Keep the shoulders relaxed and avoid an overly rigid posture, creating an effortless editorial lookbook feeling.

## 11 | Adjusting the hem pose

The model stands at approximately a 30–45 degree angle to the camera. One hand gently holds or adjusts the hem of the garment while the other arm hangs naturally. The head tilts slightly downward, with the gaze directed toward the clothing or floor. Legs are naturally staggered. Keep the hand movement subtle to highlight fabric texture, drape, construction, and hemline without stretching, lifting, or changing the perceived length.

## 12 | Relaxed cross-leg standing pose

The model stands facing the camera or at a slight three-quarter angle. One leg supports the body weight while the other crosses naturally in front, with the toe lightly touching the floor. The body weight shifts subtly to one side, creating a relaxed silhouette. Both arms may hang naturally, or one hand may lightly rest near the opposite wrist. Keep the expression calm and understated, creating a clean, sophisticated, and effortless fashion lookbook aesthetic.

## Coverage routing

- Front views: prefer `01`, `07`, `10`, or `12`.
- Side and side three-quarter views: prefer `02`, `04`, `06`, or `08`.
- Back and rear three-quarter views: prefer `03` or `09`.
- Front three-quarter and closer person views: prefer `05`, `06`, `10`, `11`, or `12`.
- Street movement views: prefer `07`, `08`, or `09`.

Keep expressions natural and understated. Avoid rigid shoulders, tense hands, exaggerated stride, fashion-runway theatricality, repeated pose geometry, and gestures that conceal product evidence.
